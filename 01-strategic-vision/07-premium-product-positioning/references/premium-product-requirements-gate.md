# Premium Product Requirements Gate

Use this gate when a product must feel premium and support high-ticket positioning.

## Buyer And Market Requirements

- Identify the economic buyer, daily user, technical approver, procurement owner, and executive sponsor.
- State the buyer's expensive problem and the measurable or perceived value of solving it.
- Define proof requirements: demo, case study, ROI estimate, security evidence, references, pilot, compliance, or governance pack.
- Specify what would make the buyer reject the product as too risky, ordinary, slow, cheap-looking, or unsupported.

## Product Requirements

- The system shall produce a high-confidence first-run experience with guided setup, sample state, clear next action, and visible support.
- The system shall include executive-level views where the buyer needs progress, ROI, risk, performance, compliance, or spend visibility.
- The system shall include branded, board-ready, exportable outputs where reports are part of the value proposition.
- The system shall support configured roles, approval paths, audit logs, service levels, and issue escalation for high-trust use cases.
- The system shall make premium service visible: onboarding plan, success milestones, review cadence, account owner, training, and support history.

## UX And Brand Requirements

- Use restrained hierarchy, polished typography, consistent spacing, calm colour, and clear language.
- Avoid clutter, generic stock imagery, vague claims, novelty UI, and slow flows.
- Treat error, empty, loading, offline, permission, and escalation states as part of the premium experience.
- For affluent/luxury-facing products, protect exclusivity, discretion, privacy, personalisation, and service feel.
- For enterprise-facing products, protect reliability, governance, auditability, integration, reporting, and risk control.

## Validation

- Test with target-level buyers, not only internal staff.
- Validate willingness to pay, buying committee concerns, sales objections, and proof gaps.
- Run a premium teardown against best alternatives, including non-software substitutes.
- Confirm the product can support the promised service level operationally and financially.
