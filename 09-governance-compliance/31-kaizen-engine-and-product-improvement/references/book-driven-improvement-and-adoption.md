# Book-driven improvement and adoption

This reference distils durable improvement, operating-system, learning-transfer, and human-agent principles from the supplied business, improvement, instructional-design, and orchestration books. It is an independent synthesis, not a source transcript.

## Improvement contract

Every improvement must state:

- the user, system, or business outcome being improved;
- the operational or product measure, including an operational definition and baseline;
- the smallest change that could plausibly affect the measure;
- the expected mechanism, risk, owner, decision rights, and rollback trigger;
- the evidence that will determine whether the change is adopted, adapted, standardised, or abandoned.

Use a short cycle: observe -> baseline -> select -> experiment -> check -> standardise -> teach -> re-measure. A proposal is not evidence, and a polished deliverable is not proof of transfer.

## Requirements as testable hypotheses

Treat requirements as claims about a user, workflow, constraint, or outcome. Record the source, confidence, assumptions, acceptance evidence, and change impact. Separate invariant controls from adaptable implementation choices. When evidence conflicts, expose the conflict and escalate the decision rather than silently averaging it away.

## Adoption and learning transfer

For any new process, capability, or tool, design the transfer path as explain -> demonstrate -> practise -> assess -> support -> observe transfer -> measure result. Capture the critical behaviour, leading indicator, manager or system driver, and result measure. Reaction or completion data may diagnose friction; it cannot stand alone as proof of capability or value.

## Human-agent decision safety

Make role ownership, autonomy limits, approval gates, side-effect budgets, escalation conditions, containment, recovery, and post-incident learning explicit. Increase autonomy only after evidence supports the next risk tier. Maintain an audit trail for material decisions and preserve a human override for consequential or ambiguous actions.

## Currentness boundary

Books may supply durable concepts and questions. They do not establish current standards, laws, policies, security controls, platform capabilities, software versions, or commands. Route those claims through the Digital Research Engine's source-evaluation and source-verification gates and record source ID, scope, publication or version date, access date, freshness class, review date, and support status.

## Review checklist

Before release, ask: Is the aim measurable? Is the baseline reproducible? Is the change reversible? Is adoption observable? Is the owner clear? Are failure and escalation paths defined? Are current claims supported by live authoritative sources? Is the evidence sufficient for the strength of the recommendation?
