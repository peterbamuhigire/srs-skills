## Rules

- Use British English by default, unless the approved product, buyer, legal, or locale brief says otherwise.
- Prefer literal, testable language. Name the actor, trigger, state, behaviour, condition, and observable outcome.
- Check the grammatical frame and collocation of important terms; do not use a thesaurus synonym merely because it sounds sophisticated.
- Use specialist terms only when necessary, defined, and consistent with the approved glossary or current authoritative source.
- Avoid idioms, jokes, figurative claims, vague intensifiers, and culturally narrow phrasing in requirements, error states, runbooks, security, accessibility, and international documentation.
- Do not use polished prose to hide an unsupported claim, missing owner, unmeasured adjective, or unresolved product decision.

## SRS review

1. Identify reader, task, system state, and decision.
2. Check each requirement for actor, trigger, behaviour, constraint, and test oracle.
3. Check terminology, collocation, grammar, spelling, identifiers, units, and cross-references.
4. Replace `fast`, `intuitive`, `seamless`, `robust`, and similar adjectives with measurable conditions.
5. Read user-facing text aloud and test it in context; retain a language/proof record.
